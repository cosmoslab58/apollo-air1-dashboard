Each channel is labelled in its own terms. The AQI words are EPA’s legally defined categories for outdoor pollutants and are only accurate in that column — EPA does not regulate indoor CO₂ at all, so the CO₂ column is labelled for ventilation instead. The two Sensirion gas indices carry no category words and stop at purple: they score against a learned ~72 h baseline, so they measure “different from recent normal”, which is not a severity claim.

“PM AQI” is the particulate-driven portion of an AQI, from the current reading. A full EPA AQI is the worst of PM2.5, PM10, ozone, NO₂, SO₂ and CO; this device measures only the two particulate channels, so on a high-ozone day the real outdoor AQI is higher than this and nothing here would know. It is also not EPA’s NowCast — that is a 12-hour weighted average, whereas this figure has no history behind it and responds immediately.

The CO₂ bands are a ventilation guide, not a health standard. No government body publishes a severity scale for indoor CO₂. Only the ends are anchored: 800 ppm is CDC ventilation guidance, 1100 ppm the ASHRAE comfort range, and 5000 ppm the OSHA permissible exposure limit — an 8-hour limit for healthy adult workers, which is not the exposure pattern of a bedroom. The 2000 and 3500 boundaries are interpolation.

The strobe is a separate alarm that outranks every colour above and only fires at levels that are genuinely harmful to breathe. It always runs at full brightness, ignoring the brightness slider. VOC is excluded from it by default because a baseline-relative spike from cleaning spray or a new package is not an emergency. Switch between the steady colours and the alarm with Alarm mode below — they are exclusive.
